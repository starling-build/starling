## Rules, and what to change
1. A mark that hangs hangs whenever its ORIGIN is inside the box. PowerPoint's margin is under 0.06 pt (slide 15: origin 120.24 kept
   in a 120.3 box; slide 1: 66.24 in 66.4; slide 3: 47.04 in 47.4). Our hang pass in RichLayout+Paragraph+Body1.swift sets the
   hung mark 0.5 pt wide (`adj[m] = -max(0, w - _px(0.5))`), so we need half a point more and break one box early on slides 1 and 3.
   Set it to zero width. This is measured, not a tolerance.
2. ")" after Hangul hangs (slide 1). ")" after kanji or kana does NOT (slide 6: it breaks as soon as its end passes the box).
   Our `isMark` takes ")" after U+3040..U+D7AF, which includes kanji and kana. Limit it to Hangul syllables before the ")".
   Caveat: this lab cannot tell "after Hangul" from "the run is ko-KR in Malgun Gothic"; both real cases (ko-4f5289a9 p6,
   ko-3d95d35b p9) are Korean, so the narrower rule is the safe one.
3. Full-width closing marks never hang: 」 (fixed by PR 1127) and ） 】 〕 》 (slides 5 and 12). Same as hang2. Adding ）】〕》 to
   `_slidesStandingUnits` is still ordered after the face fix (item 1 of the squeeze spec), because 505a93a9 depends on it.
4. ” in a ja-JP run after a kanji is set in the East Asian face: MS Gothic, a full em (slide 7). We set it in Arial, 6.7 pt, and then
   hang it. The corpus agrees: in the ja prints, ” and “ after kana or kanji are always in the run's Japanese face (MS PGothic,
   Yu Gothic, HG Maru Gothic, Meiryo), and in Arial only after Latin text (5cf98ad4). Fix the face choice for “ ” ‘ ’ in an
   East Asian-language run (check whether the run's lang or the character before decides; this lab has both).
   Then hang ” only when it is set narrow (under 0.75 em, as in slide 3); a full-width ” never hangs.
5. No squeeze anywhere (slides 12-14 break exactly at the natural width). Confirms the squeeze spec.
6. RETRACT item 6 of the squeeze spec: for "공작 ”" in 40-46 pt boxes PowerPoint ALSO puts ” alone at the start of line 2
   (its origin, 47.04, is outside the box, so it cannot hang). Ours matches. Not a bug.
