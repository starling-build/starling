# amd-opus spec (puzzle 17): Sheets wrap-fit room vs column width (for amd-3)

## Answer
The three "inconsistent" gaps do not come from one model, so they cannot be compared. Only one of them is a clean measurement. On the clean data the room is the column less a constant 11 or 12 px (192-dpi screen, Calibri 11). No width-dependent rule is needed yet.

## Why each point is or is not usable
1. col 105 (lab w7), "room exactly 91, gap 14": NOT clean. "applicable" (120 px) and "Individual" (115 px) are wider than the column, so the 7-line count depends on how Excel breaks inside a word and whether the rest of the word shares a line with the next word. amd-3 skipped widths 5 and 6 for this reason; width 7 has the same problem. With a plain model (break in the word, remainder continues) my re-run gives room 92-94, gap 11-13. The only firm fact there: "for New" (34+7+54 = 95 px) does NOT fit, "e-KYC is" (92) probably does. So gap >= 11.
2. col 160 (52716), gap 11-12: clean (ASCII, words shorter than the column, hinted table). Keep.
3. col 419 (no_drawing_patriarch), gap 19-22: NOT clean. Its wrapped cells are Russian (column C) and long URLs with no spaces (column D, the 27.78-wide one). The hinted table covers only U+0020-007E; the sim falls back to Carlito x 29.33 rounded for Cyrillic (wrong ppem, unhinted), and URL break points are unmodelled. An 8-10 px error over a 400 px line is 2%, which is the size of that fallback error. Also check the column: Excel's pixel width for 27.7778 is trunc((256*w+8)/256*15) = 417, not 419.
4. Lab upper bounds (<=14 at 180, <=21 at 240, <=24 at 300) and width 5 (9-13 in my run) all allow 11-12.
So: constant 11 or 12 fits every clean point. "0.965 x col - 6" was fitted through point 3 and should be dropped.

## What is still not known
- 11 or 12, and whether it really is constant up to wide columns (nothing clean above 160 px).
- Whether the hinted ppem-29 table is what Excel sums on long lines.
- Whether the constant scales with the screen (5 px at 96 dpi is the known figure; 11-12 at 192 dpi would be about 2 x 5 + 1 or 2). All corpus prints are from the 192-dpi box, so this does not block a fix; write the constant as a named 192-dpi value, not a fraction of the column.

## Confirming lab (built, not printed; planner decides)
lab/sheets-wrap-room/wrap-room.xlsx (on amd-opus, /data/s/amd-opus/starling/lab/sheets-wrap-room/), key in key.tsv beside it. 78 rows, one wrapped cell each, Calibri 11. Columns of 105, 161, 240, 300, 420, 600 px. In each, 13 rows whose text is exactly column - k px wide in the hinted table, k = 6..18. All ASCII, every word shorter than the column, at least two words. Column A holds the label "c<px> k<k>".
Read: a row printed one line tall (14.52 pt) means the text fitted; two lines (about 29 pt) means it did not. The largest k that is still two lines, per column, is the gap minus 1.
- Same k in all six columns: constant gap, widths table good. Land room = column - that gap.
- k grows with the column: either the gap is proportional or the table's widths are short on long lines; the 600 px column separates them (a width error shows as about 1 px per 50 px of text).
Package checked with a namespace-aware parser (ElementTree, every part).

## Do not
- Do not use rows whose words are wider than the column, non-ASCII text or URLs to fit the room. Model those only after the room is fixed, each with its own lab.
- Do not pick the constant by sweep score.
