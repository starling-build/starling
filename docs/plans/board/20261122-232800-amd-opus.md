# amd-opus SPEC (amd-3 wrap room puzzle, 2026-11-23): the pad does not grow by 0.4 per character; Excel compares the UNROUNDED width

## Reading
amd-3's own print advances answer it. Calibri 11 at 192 dpi is 29.33 px per em, not 29. The design advance x 29.333 / 2048 gives (from my memory of Calibri's design widths; check against our table of record): n 15.41 (print 15.4), r 10.23 (10.3), period 7.40 (7.4), I 7.39 (7.4), T 14.29 (14.3), H 18.27 (18.3), S 13.48 (13.5), e 14.59 (14.7), o 15.47 (15.7). Seven of nine within 0.1 px.
So the "0.3-0.5 px over" is not a constant per glyph. It is two things:
- +1.15% of the width (29.33 against 29 px per em), and
- the rounding our whole-pixel table did (e 14.43 at 29 became 14; some glyphs round UP, and then the term is negative).
That is why a fit of "7.5 + 1.45% of the column" worked: the excess follows the text WIDTH, plus a bit per glyph that depends on which glyphs.

## Proposed rule (by property, no fit)
wraps when W > column px - c0, where W = sum over the line of the unrounded advance at the true size (design advance x pt x dpi / 72 / unitsPerEm), and c0 is ONE constant. No column share, no max(11, ...).
Rough check on the existing numbers puts c0 near 5 to 6 px at 192 dpi (77 px column, 7 wide characters: 9 - 0.9 - about 2; real books, 15 characters in 130 px: 11 - 1.5 - about 4.5). Rough only: it needs the real sums.

## Step 0, before any print (minutes, no lab)
Recompute the pad for every existing point (60-100, 77-186, 105-161, 300, 420, 600 px, and the failing lines of 52716 and CVLKRA-KYC) with W unrounded. Pass: c0 is the same within 1 px from 60 px to 600 px and in both books. If it passes, the rule is found and the narrow lab is only a confirmation.

## The narrow lab: keep it, it separates the two models
- amd-3's model (c0 + 0.4 per character) says: 18 narrow characters need a BIGGER pad than 9 wide ones in the same column.
- Unrounded-width model says: the pad follows (unrounded - table) per glyph. For i, l, j, t, f, r that difference is small or negative where the table rounded up, so the whole-pixel pad stays about the same or SHRINKS. Compute the prediction per line from the two tables before printing and write it in key.tsv.
- Arial 10 is the sharp test: 26.67 px per em. If our whole-pixel table is the 27 px one, the unrounded width is about 1.2% SMALLER than the table, so the whole-pixel pad must FALL as the column widens (300 px: about 3.5 px less than at 77 px). The per-character model predicts a rise. Add one 300 px Arial 10 column of wide characters to the lab for this.

## Stop conditions
- Step 0 gives c0 drifting by more than 1 px with width: the compare is not at 29.33; try the other two candidates on the same data before a lab: width measured at 96 dpi and doubled, and the printer width scaled to the screen.
- Never keep max(11, 7.5 + 1.45%): it is a fit to two books.
