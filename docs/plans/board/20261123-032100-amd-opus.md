# amd-opus 2026-11-23: three specs (amd-3 cjk-line, amd-3 wrap-mid, amd-6 pie-inside)

Measured from the Excel / PowerPoint prints already in the labs (pdf-probe glyph origins, 2 decimals of a point). "Dots" are 600ths of an inch.

## 1. Sheets: CJK wrapped-line pitch (amd-3 cjk-line)

Proven from the two prints:
- Every pitch is one whole number of dots, the same between all lines of a cell (no 1-dot jitter). 100%: PMingLiU = MingLiU 98 109 119 130 152 172, SimSun 94 103 113 122 141 162, JhengHei 103 113 124 133 155 175 (9/10/11/12/14/16 pt). amd-3's read is exact.
- The 83% pitch is round(pitch at 100% x 0.83) in 18 of 18 cells (81 90 99 108 126 143 / 78 85 94 101 117 134 / 85 94 103 110 129 145). So Excel takes the line at the UNSCALED 600 dpi and scales the result. The 83% print holds no second sample of the font metrics: there are 6 numbers per face, not 12. Store 100% values only; scale and round to nearest.
- Not proven: the formula. Candidate A: line = tmHeight + tmExternalLeading + half of tmDescent (half rounds up), with GDI's metrics at the printer em (round(pt x 600 / 72) = 75 83 92 100 117 133). With plain rounding of the design metrics it gives PMingLiU 6 of 6 (75+15+7.5, 83+17+8.5, 92+18+9, 100+20+10, 117+23+11.5, 133+26+13), SimSun 2 of 6 (1-2 dots short at 9-12 pt). A search over rounding choices tops out at 15 of 24, so do not pick roundings by fit.
- Why the design metrics cannot settle it: GDI's own numbers are not the design ones. The 192-dpi dump has SimSun 12 pt with height 33 for em 32 (internal leading 1), and JhengHei height 1.24-1.27 em, not the 1.33 of its win metrics (and 1.25-1.33 at 96 dpi). JhengHei "equal to asc+desc at 12 and 14" is a coincidence of that. Candidate A with JhengHei's GDI height near 1.25 em gives 103 at 9 pt (93.75 + 9.5), where win metrics gave 100.

## 2. Sheets: column characters to print dots per scale (amd-3 wrap-mid)

The prints hold the columns directly (text origin of column B minus A, C minus B, D minus C; the pad cancels). Book is Calibri 11: screen digit 15 px at 192 dpi, print digit 47 dots.

| column (192-dpi px) | 100% | 81% | 66% | 50% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A 330 | 1034.2 | 838.2 | 682.2 | 517.2 |
| B 77 | 241.25 | 195.0 | 159.0 | 121.0 |
| C 147 | 461.0 | 373.25 | 304.0 | 231.0 |

- The app's rule, px x 47/15 x scale, gives 1034.0 837.5 682.4 517.0 / 241.3 195.4 159.2 120.6 / 460.6 373.1 304.0 230.3: within 0.5 dot in 11 of 12 cells. The one that is off is C at 50%: Excel 231.0, app 230.3.
- The fitted model's column, floor(px x 3.125 x scale), gives 459 372 303 229 for C and 1031 for A at 100%: 1-3 dots NARROWER than Excel everywhere. It is not Excel's column. It scored 90/90 because its "26 x scale" margin absorbed the error; that margin is a fit, not a measurement. Flooring to a whole 96-dpi px is likewise wrong (rightly reverted).
- So at 50% Excel's 147 px column is 0.7 dot WIDER than the app's, the opposite direction from the proposed fix. The 7 Arial misses (slack -0.25 to -1.25 dots) sit inside that 0.7 dot plus the margin; they are not a column-too-wide error.
- Column edges are not on whole dots (B starts at 1474.17, 1276.2, 1118.2, 951.2), so "whole dots" is not the rule either. Per-column round, floor and ceil of px x 47/15 x scale each fail one cell (ceil fails B at 100%, round and floor fail C at 50%).
- Second finding: the text inset is not proportional to scale. Column A's first glyph stands 20, 18, 16, 14 dots right of the 0.7 in margin at 100/81/66/50%: 8 + 12 x scale. Either the page origin is 8 dots in, or the cell pad has a fixed part. If the pad has a fixed part, the wrap room is column - (c + k x scale), which is exactly the shape the 50% misses need.

Lab (round 1 for this question; one book, four scales):
1. Thin borders on every cell (edges are then read directly, both sides of each column) and 12 columns: 20, 33, 47, 64, 77, 101, 147, 150, 203, 300, 301, 451 px (as (px + 0.5)/15 characters), Calibri 11 book; the same in an Arial 10 book.
2. In each column one left-aligned, one right-aligned and one centred short text, so the left and right pad are read separately at each scale.
3. Scales 100, 81, 66, 50, plus 25 and 200 to stretch any fixed part.
Read: column width per px per scale (is C at 50% a one-off or a pattern), left pad and right pad per scale. Then the room rule is column - leftPad - rightPad from measured parts, and amd-3's break key (215 rows) is the test, not the fit.
Do not change column() before this; the present rule is right to half a dot in 11 of 12.

## 3. Slides: bestFit pie labels (amd-6 pie-inside, with addenda a and b)

Measured on pie-lab7 (centre 301.6, 239.7 on every slide; R 120.2 on slides 1, 2, 5, 10; R 139 on slides 3, 4, 11):
- inEnd (slide 3) and ctr (slide 4) labels ARE on the slice bisector: 22% at 0.66 R, 2.9 deg from a 3.6 deg bisector; 15% at 0.60 R, 34.9 vs 34.2 deg; 14% at 0.70 R, 28.5 vs 28.8 deg; ctr at 0.50 R.
- The unmoved bestFit inside labels (slide 1) are NOT on the bisector. Ink-box centres from the pie centre: 22% (+75.8, +15.8), 0.64 R, 15 deg clockwise of its bisector; 15% (-70.5, +55.6), 0.75 R, 4 deg off; 14% four-line (+31.5, +86.9), 0.77 R, 9 deg off. All three are displaced toward the bottom of the chart. (Block centre y taken as the middle baseline less 4 pt, so +-2 pt.)
- So "which test on the bisector lets the box inside" is the wrong question: PowerPoint does not put these boxes on the bisector. They are not the inEnd position, nor the inEnd position scaled by 120.2/139, nor the outEnd position (slide 2) shifted. A fit test on box, angle and radius should not be written until the position is known; no tolerance on "corners inside wedge".
- Addendum (b), from the same lab: slide 5 moves every bestFit label by (0.05, 0.05) = (21, 15) pt. The 22% label, which sits INSIDE when unmoved, lands at x 430.6, first baseline 229.2 = its outEnd position on slide 2 (430.6, 214.2) plus 15 pt down, x held at the chart's right edge. So for a moved bestFit label the base is outside-end even when the unmoved label would be inside. "Base is the inside-fit position when the slice is wide" is ruled out for this pie. ja-5ed7b5ff96's 16 pt needs another cause; first suspects are the plot radius (with no outside label the plot is not shrunk: R 139 against 120.2 here) and the x clamp at the chart edge.
